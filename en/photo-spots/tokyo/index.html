Tokyo photo spots: Asakusa, Shibuya and city nights

Neighbourhood and time of day shape a Tokyo photoshoot. Start by choosing everyday street scenes, traditional architecture or the colours of the city at night. Each photographed location below links to the map, where you can explore more images from that place.

Sensō-ji

Use the Sensō-ji photographs as a reference for portraits with traditional architecture. Nearby streets can also be discussed as options, with the day’s pedestrian traffic guiding the places where we stop.

Shibuya Scramble Crossing

Look at the Shibuya Crossing images if you enjoy the movement of the city. When choosing references, consider whether you prefer a clear portrait or a wider frame in which the person and street share attention.

Kabukichō Ichibangai Gate

The Kabukichō entrance images can help communicate the neon colours you want in a night shoot. Share your preferred finishing time as well, allowing room for your journey back or plans later that evening.

Tokyo Station

Compare the Tokyo Station photographs with Asakusa and Shibuya to choose an architectural setting that suits your clothing. Once the visual direction is clear, we can discuss nearby stops and timing without overcrowding the itinerary.

View photos on the map ↗

Planning your shoot

For a couple shoot, a walk through one neighbourhood leaves room for interaction. Solo travellers can use a few favourite images to discuss clothing and atmosphere. For families, start with meeting points, walking distance and breaks. Discuss daylight and night portraits separately before combining distant neighbourhoods.

Before your visit

Share your accommodation area, available time and any kimono or wedding-dress plans. Shops, observation decks and venues may have their own entry, booking and photography rules, which need checking for the visit. A map marker identifies where a photograph was taken.
